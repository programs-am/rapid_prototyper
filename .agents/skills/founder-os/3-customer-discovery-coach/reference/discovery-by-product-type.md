# Discovery by Product Type

Load this once, early, based on the founder's product type (company ground truth,
`02_Product`, field "Product type"). If the field is empty or mixed, ask which of the
types below is closest and use that one — or two, if the product is a hybrid (a smart water
bottle is hardware plus software). Use it to flavor the hypothesis questions and, later, the
interview guide: change what the founder observes and what they ask, but keep every Mom Test
rule intact.

Throughout: observe behavior first, ask about the past second, never ask about the future.

---

## Software (apps, platforms, internal tools)

**Observe:** the current workflow screen by screen, tabs left open, spreadsheets and
copy-paste steps, who gets pinged for help, what is done by hand every week.
**Ask about:** the last time the task happened, what tools were involved, what went wrong.

1. "Walk me through the last time you had to [task]. What did you open first?"
2. "Which tools or spreadsheets did you touch, and where did you get stuck?"
3. "Who else had to be involved before it was done?"
4. "What did you try before that, and why did you stop using it?"
5. "When did you last pay for something to make this easier?"

**Stated vs revealed trap:** "I'd definitely use that." People say yes to features, then never
change a habit. Revealed signal: they already built a workaround, pay for a patch, or lose
real time every week.

---

## Website and marketing (sites, campaigns, comms, brand)

**Observe:** how people actually arrive (search, referral, social), where they hesitate on a
page, what they screenshot or forward, which of your existing messages gets replies.
**Ask about:** the last time they looked for a provider or option like yours, and how they
chose between them.

1. "Think of the last time you looked for [a provider / product like this]. Where did you start?"
2. "What made you close a tab or skip a message?"
3. "Who did you ask or forward it to before deciding?"
4. "What did you need to see or read before you trusted them?"
5. "What did the last one you picked get right or wrong?"

**Stated vs revealed trap:** "The design looks great" and "I'd sign up." Compliments cost
nothing. Revealed signal: they click, reply, forward, or hand over an email address or time.

## Physical product and hardware (devices, tools, industrial components)

**Observe (contextual inquiry):** visit the place of use and watch the task being done with
today's solution: grip, posture, space, noise, cleaning, storage, where it breaks.
**Teardown of the current solution:** buy or borrow what they use now; note materials, parts
count, failure points, and what is annoying to assemble or maintain.
**Installer and channel interviews:** talk to the people who fit, repair, stock, or recommend
the product. They often decide more than the end user.

1. "Show me how you do this today. Can you do it right now while I watch?"
2. "When did the current one last fail or annoy you? What did you do about it?"
3. "Where is it kept, and who else touches it?"
4. "What did you have to buy, fix, or replace in the last year?"
5. (Installer or supplier) "What do you get called about most often? What do you refuse to stock or fit?"

**Stated vs revealed trap:** "I would pay more for something better built." Revealed signal:
what they bought last time, what they repaired instead of replacing, and what they put up with
every day. Watch for use-environment surprises (wet hands, gloves).

## CPG: food, beverage, beauty, home

**Observe:**
- **Shop-alongs:** walk the aisle with a shopper and watch what they pick up, flip over, and
  put back. Note what they read first (price, claims, ingredients).
- **Shelf intercepts:** short, permission-based chats at the shelf about the last purchase.
- **Pantry, fridge, or bathroom-shelf audits:** with the person's okay, look at what is
  actually there, half-used, or hidden at the back.
- **Taste and sample tests:** blind comparisons against what they buy today, with no pitch.
- **Retailer-buyer interviews:** what sells, what gets delisted, margin and slotting realities.

1. "Show me what you bought the last time you needed [category]. Why that one?"
2. "What did you put back on the shelf, and what made you put it back?"
3. "What is in your cupboard right now that you bought once and never finished?"
4. "Who else in the home eats or uses this, and who decides what gets bought?"
5. (Retailer buyer) "Think of the last new product you took on. What made you say yes, and what happened after a few weeks?"

**Stated vs revealed trap:** "I'd buy that" and "I care about clean ingredients." Shoppers
say they want healthy, then buy on price, habit, and taste. Revealed signal: what is in the
basket, the repeat purchase, and what they finish. Sampling friends and family inflates scores;
use strangers where you can.

## Fashion and apparel

**Observe:**
- **Closet audits:** what is worn weekly, what has tags on, what is kept but never worn.
- **Wear tests:** lend a sample for two weeks; ask for photos and notes at the start, after one week, and after two.
- **Fit sessions:** watch them try it on, where they tug, fold, or hesitate.
- **Resale-market signals:** what holds value on resale sites, which labels sell out, what
  size runs disappear first.

1. "Show me the things you wear most. What do they have in common?"
2. "What is the last thing you bought and never wore? What went wrong?"
3. "Where do you usually shop for [item], and how do you decide on size?"
4. "What did you return last time, and why?"
5. "Who do you ask, or what do you check, before buying something new?"

**Stated vs revealed trap:** "I love it" at a pop-up or in a group chat. Revealed signal: they
wear it, size up or down, wash it and still like it, and would be seen in it. Friends flatter;
fit and comfort verdicts after two weeks of real wear matter more than first impressions.

## Services (marketplaces, local services, consulting, B2B services)

**Observe:**
- **Shadowing:** follow a customer or provider through a full job: scheduling, back-and-forth,
  payment, follow-up, and the no-shows in between.
- **Concierge pilots:** deliver the service by hand to a few customers and note every step you
  perform manually. Those steps are the product.

1. "Tell me about the last time you needed [service]. How did you find someone?"
2. "How many people did you contact, and what happened with each?"
3. "What went wrong between booking and the job being done?"
4. "How did you decide on price, and what did you end up paying?"
5. (Provider) "How do you get new clients today, and what does a bad client cost you?"

**Stated vs revealed trap:** "I'd use a service like that." Revealed signal: they already
hire someone, ask friends, or have a spreadsheet of contacts. If they have never paid anyone
to solve it, treat "I'd pay" with suspicion. Remember marketplaces have two sides; interview
both.

## Using this with the coach

- Pick the matching type before the hypothesis questions so wording like "workaround" fits
  ("what did you put back on the shelf" is a CPG workaround; "which tab do you keep open" is
  software).
- When the founder asks who to talk to, include the non-obvious roles: installers, retailer
  buyers, gift givers, store staff, dispatchers, procurement.
- In the final guide, add one "observe, don't ask" line per Critical hypothesis where the type
  allows it. For hybrids, lead with the type where the riskiest hypothesis lives.
