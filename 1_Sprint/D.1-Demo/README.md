# D.1-Demo

**Purpose:** present and defend the chain, **jobs, then prototype, then metrics**, in about 9 minutes to someone who can say yes, no, or "next." If AI is part of your product, add one more link: what role it plays and why that role is defensible. If it is not, skip it.

**Typical time:** about 9 minutes presenting plus 10-20 minutes of questions. Allow 1-2 hours to prepare.

## Inputs

- Your ranked jobs (A.4)
- PRD v2 and your latest prototype (C.3)
- Your Objective and Key Results (C.2)
- What you learned from the retro, advisor review, and peer review

## What you do by hand

- Choose the audience: a prospective customer, a buyer, an advisor, an investor, a partner, or a panel of peers. The test is that they can answer yes, no, or next.
- Present the chain in order: the job your customer is trying to get done, the prototype and what it shows about your riskiest assumption, and the metrics that will tell you whether the bet is paying off. Add the AI role only if AI is in the product.
- Be ready to defend each link. Useful questions to ask yourself or a friendly reviewer:
  - Is the job clear and solution-agnostic?
  - Did you prioritize and kill, or did you keep everything?
  - Does the prototype test the riskiest assumption?
  - Do the metrics make sense for that assumption?
  - If AI is in the product, is its role defensible?
- Close with what you learned, what you will do next, and the one thing you are asking for.
- Afterward, use `5_Library/roadmap/Roadmapping-Guide.md` to turn feedback into your next Now list, and ask your agent for help with the prototype-to-production step if that is where you are headed.

There is no template for this stage. A short slide deck or one-page brief is plenty.

## What the skill writes

No pipeline skill runs at this stage. Any derived notes go to `2_Outputs/.agents/D.1-Demo/`.

## Done when

- You got a yes, a no, or a next step from your audience.
- You have a written next-cycle commitment and the start of a roadmap.
