# A.3-User-Persona

**Purpose:** define one ideal customer profile: one person, one situation, one high-value need. The persona is solution-agnostic (a need, not a feature request) and seeds the JTBD stage with its Situation and Need fields.

**Typical time:** 20-30 minutes. Do it right after the value proposition (`A.3-Value-Prop/`).

## Inputs

- Your completed value proposition
- Processed evidence in `5_Library/sources/processed/` (from A.2), if you have it
- Real quotes and behavior from customer conversations

## What you do by hand

- Open `A.3-User-Persona-Template.md` (or the `.docx` version) and fill in the seven fields: Identity, Situation, Need, Current behavior, Key quote, Why this ICP, Out of scope.
- If the person who pays is not the person who uses the product (a parent, a procurement manager, a retail buyer, a client), add the optional line **Buyer, if different from the user**.
- Pick one person. If you find yourself writing "and also," you have two personas; choose one for now and put the other in Out of scope.

## What the skill writes

`user-persona` coaches the first three fields and pulls Current behavior and Key quote from your discovery data. It writes its derived deliverable to `2_Outputs/.agents/A.3-User-Persona/User-Persona.md`. It does not fill in your template.

## Done when

- The Need field describes an outcome in plain language, not a feature.
- Your Key quote is a verbatim line from a real person.
- Out of scope names who you are not building for yet.

Your completed template stays in this folder. The blank master is mirrored in `5_Library/templates/`.
