# A.3-Value-Prop

**Purpose:** leave with one sentence that says who the customer is, what problem they have, what your product does about it, and why that beats what they do today. The sentence plus a four-field block (Customer, Problem, Solution, Differentiator) narrows the space of jobs before A.4. Works for any kind of product: a packaged snack, a smart bottle, a streetwear label, a local-services marketplace, an industrial component, or software.

**Typical time:** 20-30 minutes. Do it together with the persona (`A.3-User-Persona/`) for a single 45-60 minute block.

## Inputs

- Processed evidence in `5_Library/sources/processed/` (from A.2), if you have it
- Your `0_Company/` files from A.1
- What customers have actually told you

## What you do by hand

- Open `A.3-Value-Prop-Template.md` (or the `.docx` version) and write the one-sentence value prop: **[Product] helps [person] who [problem], by [solution], unlike [alternative].** Examples across industries are in a comment at the top of the template.
- Fill in the four components: Customer, Problem, Solution, Differentiator. Be specific: "the segment," not "everyone."
- Keep the alternative honest. For most products the alternative is a workaround or doing nothing, not a named competitor.

## What the skill writes

`value-proposition` walks you through the exercise one section at a time, grounded in your evidence, and writes its derived deliverable to `2_Outputs/.agents/A.3-Value-Prop/Value-Proposition.md`. It does not fill in your template.

## Done when

- The sentence names a specific person, a specific problem, and a specific alternative.
- You can say what evidence supports each of the four components, and flag which are guesses.

Your completed template stays in this folder. The blank master is mirrored in `5_Library/templates/`.
