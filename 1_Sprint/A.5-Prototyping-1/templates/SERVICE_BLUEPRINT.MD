# SERVICE_BLUEPRINT.MD — The Service, Step by Step

Maps your service from the customer's side and from behind the curtain. For each step it shows what the customer does, what they see and touch (front-stage), what you actually do by hand to make it happen (back-stage), how long it takes, what is faked, where it can fail, and where it will break if you get more customers. This is how you prototype a service, workflow, marketplace, event or concierge offer without building the system first.

**Status:** Required for the service-experience lane. Not used for other lanes.

**Use it when:** The thing you are selling is an experience or outcome delivered over time (a service, a workflow, a matching or marketplace idea, an event, a concierge or "Wizard of Oz" test). In a Wizard of Oz test the customer sees what looks like a finished product while a person does the work by hand behind the scenes. Fill it in after PRD.MD and BUILD_PLAN.MD.

<!-- guidance: Do the work manually first. Doing it by hand teaches you what customers actually need and which steps are worth automating later. Be honest in the "faked / manual" column: it protects both you and your testers. -->

**Prototype guardrails:** A prototype stores no permanently-stored sensitive personal data and takes no real payments. (A consented email waitlist through a reputable form tool is the one allowed exception.) Do not collect health, financial, government-ID or other sensitive details. Never present a mocked or manually run service as a finished, safety-tested, licensed or compliant one.

**Honest disclosure rules for concierge and Wizard of Oz tests:**

- Tell participants beforehand that this is an early test of a new service, even if you do not explain every behind-the-scenes detail. You may hide how it works, but not that it is a test.
- Never claim automation, AI, certification, licensing or expertise you do not have.
- Never invent customers, reviews or results to make the service look established.
- Do not promise outcomes, delivery dates or quality you cannot personally deliver.
- If a step could affect someone's health, money, safety or legal position, verify with a qualified professional before running it with real people, or do not run that step.
- Let participants stop at any time, and tell them how their information will be used and deleted.

## Ask the LLM

Paste these into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini one at a time. Share your PRD.MD first so the AI has context.

1. "I shared my PRD. List the steps a customer goes through from first hearing about my service to after it ends. Keep each step to something the customer notices. Aim for 5 to 10 steps. Show them as a numbered list I can paste into a table."

2. "For my first three steps, describe what the customer sees and touches (front-stage) and what I would have to do by hand behind the scenes (back-stage). Be specific about tools, messages and who does what."

3. "For each step, tell me where it is most likely to fail, how long it will take me by hand, and what I do if it fails. Then tell me at what number of customers each step would break, and why."

4. "Mark which steps in my blueprint are real, which are faked or done manually, and write an honest line I could use if a customer asks 'is this automated?'. Remind me of the disclosure rules for a concierge or Wizard of Oz test, and flag any step that touches health, money, safety or legal matters, which I should verify with a qualified professional."

5. "Write a one-page call script for the first conversation with a customer: opening that says this is an early test, three open questions, what happens next, how to answer 'can I trust this?' honestly, and a closing."

6. "Draft a short intake form with only the questions I need to deliver the test, and nothing sensitive. For each, say why I need it. Write a one-sentence consent line that says how I will use and delete the answers."

7. "Write a confirmation message and a reminder message that are friendly and honest, say this is an early test, avoid overpromising, and use [PLACEHOLDER] for any detail I have not decided."

8. "Review my completed blueprint. Tell me which step is most likely to break first, how many customers I can realistically serve manually, which manual step I should automate first, and any place where I might be misleading a customer."

## Done when

- [ ] Every step in the journey has a customer action, a front-stage description and a back-stage description.
- [ ] Every faked or manual step is labeled, and you have a truthful answer ready if a customer asks about it.
- [ ] Time per step is estimated, and total hours per customer are known.
- [ ] Failure points and a fallback are written for the riskiest steps.
- [ ] The "scale breaks here" column is filled in for every step.
- [ ] The number of customers you will serve in this test is capped.
- [ ] The script, intake form, confirmation message and feedback questions are written.
- [ ] The intake form collects no sensitive personal data and states how answers will be used and deleted.
- [ ] No real payments are taken, nothing is presented as automated, licensed or certified if it is not, and any step touching health, money, safety or legal matters has been checked with a qualified professional or removed.
