# CAMPAIGN.MD — Campaign Plan & Test Design

Defines a small, testable marketing or communications effort: the single thing you want to learn, who you are talking to, what you say, where you say it, what assets you need, the sequence, how you will judge it, and what it costs. A campaign prototype is not a full launch; it is the smallest live or simulated run that tells you whether a message, offer or channel works.

**Status:** Required for the marketing-comms lane. Optional as a companion for website or cpg prototypes that include a launch or waitlist test.

**Use it when:** You are testing a message, brand idea, offer, email or social sequence, deck or pitch kit with real people. Fill it in after PRD.MD and BUILD_PLAN.MD. Work with COPY.MD for the words and DESIGN.MD for the look.

<!-- guidance: One campaign, one hypothesis, at most two channels. If you cannot say in one sentence what you will learn, the campaign is too big. A prototype campaign should be cheap enough that a failure costs you a little money and teaches you a lot. -->

**Prototype guardrails:** A prototype stores no permanently-stored sensitive personal data and takes no real payments. (A consented email waitlist through a reputable form tool is the one allowed exception.) Do not present mockups, samples or placeholders as a finished, safety-tested or compliant product, and do not collect money or make firm delivery promises as part of this test.

## Compliance & disclosure checks

<!-- guidance: These are questions to verify, not statements of what any law or platform requires. Rules vary by country, platform and product category, and they change. Check each with the platform's current published policy and, where money or health is involved, a qualified professional. -->

- [ ] What are the current advertising rules of each platform I will use for my product category (restricted or prohibited categories, targeting limits, landing-page rules)?
- [ ] If anyone is paid or given product to post about it: what endorsement or sponsorship disclosure rules apply in my market, and how must it be shown?
- [ ] Is every claim in the copy supported by evidence I hold? (See the claims table in COPY.MD.) Which claims need a professional review?
- [ ] Email and messaging: what consent and unsubscribe rules apply where my audience lives? Do I have clear opt-in and a way to opt out?
- [ ] Am I using images, music, fonts or logos I have the right to use?
- [ ] Am I using people's names, faces or words with their permission? (No invented testimonials.)
- [ ] Any contest, giveaway or discount: what rules apply to running it?
- [ ] Privacy: what do I collect, where is it stored, and does my sign-up form say what I will do with it?

## Ask the LLM

Paste these into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini one at a time. Share your PRD.MD and (if you have it) COPY.MD first so the AI has context.

1. "I shared my PRD. Help me write a single testable hypothesis for a small marketing campaign in this format: 'If we do X for audience Y, then Z will happen, because R.' Give me three options from safest to boldest and tell me which assumption each one tests."

2. "Describe where my target user already spends attention online and offline. Suggest the two best channels for a cheap prototype test, and tell me what I would give up by not using the others. Do not suggest buying or scraping contact lists."

3. "Write the core message and offer for my campaign in one sentence each, using my COPY.MD. Then draft the call to action and list three things I must not claim because I cannot support them."

4. "Create an asset list for my campaign with format, channel, owner and status. Keep it to the minimum needed to run the test, and flag any asset that is expensive that I could fake or simplify."

5. "Build a day-by-day sequence (Day 1, Day 3, etc.) for a [length]-day run on [channel], including setup before Day 1 and a review step at the end. Do not use calendar dates."

6. "Design an A/B test for this hypothesis. Tell me what single thing to vary, how to split a small audience, what success threshold to set before launch, and what I can and cannot conclude from a small sample."

7. "List the leading signals I can measure without a big budget: replies, clicks, saves, sign-ups and pre-order intent. For each, tell me how to count it, and warn me about any vanity number that could mislead me."

8. "List the compliance and disclosure questions I should verify for a campaign on [platforms] about [product category] aimed at [market]: ad platform rules, endorsement disclosure, claims, and email consent. Phrase each as a question to check with the platform's current policy or a qualified professional, not as a statement of what the law requires."

## Done when

- [ ] One goal and one falsifiable hypothesis are written, and you know what decision the test informs.
- [ ] The audience is one specific group, and you know where they already spend attention.
- [ ] No more than two channels are chosen, with a reason for each.
- [ ] Every asset has a format, owner and status.
- [ ] The sequence uses relative days and includes setup and review.
- [ ] The test varies one thing, and the success threshold was set before launch.
- [ ] A budget cap is set, and no cost is invented.
- [ ] Measurement uses leading signals you can actually count, and the record location is named.
- [ ] Compliance and disclosure questions are written down and assigned to the right source (platform policy or a named kind of professional).
- [ ] The campaign takes no real payments, does not use invented testimonials or numbers, and does not present anything as finished or compliant.
