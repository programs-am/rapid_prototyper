# BUILD_PLAN.MD — How I Will Make the Prototype

Tells you, and anyone helping you, how the prototype will actually get made: what form it takes, which tools and materials you will use, who does what, what it costs, how long it takes, and how it reaches testers. This is the non-software equivalent of TECH_STACK.MD. It keeps you from buying the wrong materials, hiring too early, or building a polished sample when a cheap mockup would answer your question.

**Status:** Required for every lane except software-app. (Software-app prototypes use TECH_STACK.MD instead.)

**Use it when:** You are building a website, a physical product, a CPG product (food, beverage, beauty, personal care, household), a fashion item, a marketing or comms piece, or a service experience. Fill it in right after PRD.MD and before you spend any money or prompt any tool.

<!-- guidance: A prototype is the cheapest thing that can answer your riskiest question. Before you fill anything in, write that question at the top of "Prototype at a glance". Every choice below should be judged against it. -->

**Prototype guardrails:** A prototype stores no permanently-stored sensitive personal data and takes no real payments. (A consented email waitlist through a reputable form tool is the one allowed exception.) Mockups and samples must never be presented to testers as finished, safety-tested or compliant products, and nothing you make here is cleared for real sale.

## What this build is NOT

<!-- guidance: Read this section before you share your prototype with anyone. These boundaries hold for every lane. Add lane-specific ones below. -->

- **Not production.** It is made to test a concept with a few people, not to be sold, scaled or manufactured.
- **Not safety-tested or compliant.** A sample, mockup or pilot has not been cleared for safety, labeling, claims or regulation in any market. Never tell testers otherwise.
- **Not a place for real sensitive data.** Do not collect or permanently store passwords, payment information, government IDs, health records or other sensitive personal data.
- **Not a payment processor.** No real charges. If price is part of what you are testing, ask about willingness to pay or take a non-binding pre-order intent without collecting money. If you want real money, that is a separate, later step with legal and tax advice.
- **Not a promise to customers.** A waitlist or pre-order-interest list is not a commitment to deliver. Say so on the page.
- **Not a final brand or final spec.** Names, logos, materials and costs here can all change.

## Ask the LLM

Paste these into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini one at a time. Share your PRD.MD first so the AI has context.

1. "I shared my PRD. I think my lane is [website / physical-product / cpg / fashion / marketing-comms / service-experience]. Based on the PRD, confirm or correct my lane. Then name the single riskiest assumption in my idea, and suggest the cheapest prototype that could test it."

2. "Explain these five fidelity stages in plain English for my kind of product: paper sketch, looks-like mockup, works-like prototype, looks-like and works-like together, small pilot. Which stage should I stop at to test [my riskiest question], and what would I be wasting money on if I went one stage higher?"

3. "List the tools, materials, parts or services I would need to make a [stage] prototype of [my product], for someone with no experience and a budget cap of [amount]. For each, tell me what it is for, whether there is a free or cheap option, and what real thing it stands in for. Do not invent prices; give ranges only if you are confident and say what to verify with a real quote."

4. "Break my build into pieces of work. For each, tell me whether a non-expert can do it themselves with AI help, or whether I should hire a freelancer, workshop, kitchen, printer or agency. For anything outsourced, write the short brief I would send to ask for a quote, including the questions I should ask about minimum order, lead time and revisions."

5. "Build me a milestone timeline for this prototype, in elapsed time rather than dates. Include waiting time for vendors, shipping and approvals, and one round of rework. Show me which step is the longest and what I can do in parallel."

6. "Plan how my prototype gets to [number] testers. Cover delivery, what I tell them it is, how I collect feedback, and which safety or legal questions I must verify with a qualified professional before handing it to a person. Remind me what a prototype must not do (take real payments, store sensitive data, be presented as finished or compliant)."

7. "Play a skeptical advisor. Read my BUILD_PLAN and list the five most likely ways this build goes over budget, runs late or fails to answer my riskiest question. For each, suggest a cheaper fallback."

8. "Review my completed BUILD_PLAN.MD and tell me: (a) anything that contradicts my PRD, (b) any step where I am spending more than the question requires, (c) anything missing that I will regret on test day."

## Done when

- [ ] The riskiest question and the fidelity stage you will stop at are written down, with a reason.
- [ ] "What done looks like" is a list a stranger could check yes or no.
- [ ] Every tool or material has a purpose and, where relevant, a named stand-in.
- [ ] Every piece of work is marked DIY or outsourced, and quotes have been requested for the outsourced pieces.
- [ ] A budget cap is set and the table is filled in (unknown figures marked "unknown", not guessed).
- [ ] The timeline has milestones, includes waits you do not control, and names the longest one.
- [ ] You know how the artifact reaches testers, what you will call it, and which safety or legal checks to verify with a qualified professional.
- [ ] At least three risks have a fallback.
- [ ] You have confirmed: no real payments, no permanently-stored sensitive personal data, and nothing will be presented as finished, safety-tested or compliant.
